About

Connecting quantum computation
with questions in physics.

I am a Physics PhD candidate at Florida State University and a graduate researcher with the FSU Quantum Initiative. My research spans quantum algorithms, quantum error correction, and topological quantum systems.

I work on quantum approaches to nonlinear partial differential equations and the reconstruction of fluid dynamics from sparse measurements. Alongside this, I study error correction and state preparation in topological codes.

Earlier research experience

National High Magnetic Field Laboratory

Electron magnetic resonance at MagLab

Graduate Research Assistant · Electron Magnetic Resonance Laboratory

At MagLab, I worked on electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) and molecular spin systems. My projects included clock transitions in molecular magnetism, machine learning for extracting physical properties from EPR spectra, and a high-field continuous-wave EPR cavity with in situ two-axis rotation.

I presented the cavity work as a poster at the 75th Southeastern Regional Meeting of the American Chemical Society (SERMACS) in Atlanta in 2024.

Ho Chi Minh City University of Education (HCMUE)

Excitons in two-dimensional materials

Key Laboratory of Computational Physics

My earlier work focused on excitons in monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenides, including analytical and numerical methods for energy spectra and magnetic-field effects.
